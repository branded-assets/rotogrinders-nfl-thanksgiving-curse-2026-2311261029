Methodology

The index measures specific circumstances surrounding NFL Thanksgiving Day defeats, rather than the biggest losses by final score.

Period and game selection

The study covers 27 NFL seasons from 1999 through 2025. It includes the 73 games actually played on Thanksgiving Day, the fourth Thursday in November: two games per year from 1999 through 2005 and generally three per year from 2006 through 2025. Only two were played on Thanksgiving in 2020 because Ravens–Steelers was postponed. Each game is evaluated from both teams’ perspectives, for 146 team-game records.

How the curse score is built

Only losses can add curse points. The index uses six equal-weight loss conditions. Every condition is worth one point, so there are no weighted metrics and no single condition counts more than another.

1Late lead lost

The opponent took the lead for good with 2:00 or less left in the fourth quarter or in overtime.

2Close defeat

The team lost by three points or fewer.

3Upset loss

The team lost while favored by at least three points based on the pregame Vegas line.

4Blown 14-point lead

The team lost after leading by 14 points or more at any point in the game.

5Won turnovers, still lost

The team forced more turnovers than it committed and still lost.

6Won yardage, still lost

The team gained more offensive yards than its opponent and still lost.

Equal turnovers or equal yardage do not qualify for conditions five and six. A game-tying score by itself does not qualify as a late lead change.

Scoring and ties

Each qualifying condition contributes one point, so a single game may contribute multiple points. The index ranks teams by the total number of qualifying conditions in descending order. Teams with equal totals share a rank and appear alphabetically. The separate “Distinct qualifying losses” column counts each qualifying game once, no matter how many conditions it meets.

Franchise names and participation

Franchise histories are combined under current names, including the Oakland and Las Vegas Raiders and Washington’s former team names. The Cleveland Browns, Jacksonville Jaguars, and Los Angeles Rams did not play Thanksgiving games in the 1999–2025 sample; they appear in the complete table without a rank.

Sources and verification

The research uses Pro-Football-Reference season schedules and individual game box scores for dates, outcomes, scoring progression, pregame Vegas lines, offensive yards, and turnovers. The recorded team-game data were cross-checked against scoring summaries and team statistics; the six conditions were calculated from those records.

Interpretation and limitations

These are raw cumulative counts, not percentages or rates adjusted for appearances. Dallas and Detroit host Thanksgiving games annually and therefore have more opportunities to accumulate points. The index measures selected circumstances around defeats, not team quality, the financial impact of a loss, or which defeat fans considered most painful. The study ends with the 2025 Thanksgiving games and does not include games played afterward.
